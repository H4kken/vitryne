Vitryne opens three kinds of files:

- **A DXF pattern** (`.dxf`) in the DXF-AAMA format, the pattern exchange format most pattern
  software can save to. You get its pieces, ready to place on the body and sew.
- **A Valentina or Seamly2D pattern** (`.val` or `.sm2d`), with the measurements file it was
  drafted from (`.vit` or `.vst`; `.smis` or `.smms` from Seamly2D). You get its pieces at the
  size you choose, curves and all.
- **A Vitryne project** (`.vtry`), exported from Vitryne. It opens as a new project, exactly as
  it was saved.

## Where to import from

- On the start screen, choose **Import a file**, or drop the file anywhere on the page.
- In an empty garment, choose **Import** on the _Draw your first piece_ card.
- Drop a file on the pattern or the 3D view of an open project.

## Valentina and Seamly2D patterns

A Valentina or Seamly2D pattern doesn't hold its pieces' sizes: Valentina works them out from body
measurements kept in a separate file. So Vitryne needs that file too.

- Drop the pattern and its measurements file (`.vit`, `.vst`, `.smis` or `.smms`) together, or
  import the pattern and choose **Add …** in
  the **Measurements** box: the dialog names the file the pattern was drafted with.
- With a size chart (`.vst` or `.smms`), pick the size in the same box. The pieces update.
- A pattern that needs no measurements file opens straight away.

If the app can't work out a piece in a way Vitryne can follow, the dialog lists it under
**Left out of the file** with the reason, and the other pieces still import.

Pieces that are left out today:

- **Pieces made with the union tool**, which joins two pieces into one. Not supported yet.
- **Pieces whose outline crosses or touches itself**, such as a path whose points are in the
  wrong order. Vitryne closes simple cuts, fills in a dart drawn as a hole joined to the edge by a
  cut (keeping its outline as a line), and turns two parts that touch at one point into two
  pieces ("Front (1)", "Front (2)"); anything else can't be cut or draped as drawn.
- Elliptical arcs.

## Place the pieces

Importing is one step: drop the file and the pieces open in the editor, paused. A drafted
pattern first asks for its measurements file, and with a project open you choose whether the
pieces join it or make a new project. Nothing else is asked.

Vitryne puts each piece where its name suggests (a piece called "sleeve" goes on the arms, a
"front" on the body front). The pieces no name fits wait in the **Pieces** panel, under **Not
placed**, and the status pill says how many. Place them there:

- **Drag** a piece onto the body, shown front and back side by side (torso, an arm or a leg), or
  **click** the piece and then that part of the body. A piece cut twice goes on both arms or both
  legs.
- **Hold** a piece over an arm or a leg for a second and the limb splits into finer places
  (upper arm, forearm, wrist, knee, ankle): drop it on one and the piece's top edge starts there,
  for a cuff at the wrist or a trouser leg from the knee.
- Use the place list on the piece for the other places (the head, on top of another piece).
- **✕** sets a piece aside (interfacing, facings): it stays in the project, out of the 3D drape,
  under **Set aside**, with **Bring back**.

Pieces you place or set aside are one undoable edit each. You can move the pieces afterwards by
dragging the handles in the 3D view.

Anything in the file Vitryne could not use (a piece whose outline crosses itself, extra layers,
entities it does not read) is listed in a message once the import is done.

## After the import

The pieces are placed but **not sewn yet** (these patterns carry no sewing). Sew them as in
[Your first Pattern](/guide/first-pattern#sew-the-sides): **Suggest seams** in the **Pieces**
panel finds the obvious ones, and **Sew two edges** joins the rest.

<Callout title="Already have the sewing?">
  A `garment.toml` file written for a pattern can come with it: drop both files
  together, or choose **Add a garment.toml…** in the dialog. Its seams are sewn
  on import. Vitryne's three example garments are made this way.
</Callout>

## If a file won't open

- Check that it is saved as **DXF-AAMA** (sometimes called _AAMA_ or _ASTM/AAMA_ in the save
  dialog), not a plain drawing DXF.
- The dialog says why a file couldn't be read. Curves in the file come in as short straight
  edges; you can [curve them again](/guide/first-pattern#change-a-pieces-shape).
